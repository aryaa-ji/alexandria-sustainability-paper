# A Thriving Alexandria – Where Sustainability Meets Progress

**Group seminar paper** on the urban sustainability of Alexandria, Egypt. The paper looks at the city's main challenges and proposes goals and actions in six thematic sections, using examples from other cities around the world.

📄 **Read the paper:** [Alexandria_Seminar_Paper.pdf](Alexandria_Seminar_Paper.pdf)

---

## Overview

Alexandria is Egypt's second-largest city and its main seaport, home to around 5.5 million people on a narrow coastal strip between the Mediterranean Sea and Lake Mariout. The section gives key indicators for Egypt (environment, economy, health and education, disaster risk and governance) and introduces the city's climate, economy and main challenges.

## Vision

A resilient, inclusive and eco-friendly Alexandria. The vision brings together the targets of all sections: more green space, a stronger public transport system, more renewable energy, upgraded informal neighbourhoods and fair access to housing, transport and green spaces.

## Urban Planning

**Challenges:** informal settlements with poor infrastructure, weak enforcement of building height limits, flooding and sea level rise along the coast, pollution of Lake Mariout, and conflicts between industry, housing and heritage areas.

**Proposals:**
- Upgrade informal settlements instead of demolishing them: water, sewage, electricity, roads and legal home ownership
- Extend seawalls and breakwaters, enforce coastal building setbacks and install flood monitoring
- Expand stormwater drainage and rehabilitate Lake Mariout
- Increase desalination capacity and urban green space

**Examples:** slum upgrading in São Paulo, Manshiat Nasser in Cairo, Bishan Park in Singapore, sea defences in the Netherlands.

## Green Infrastructure

**Challenges:** very little green space per person, few residents living close to a park, and loss of green areas to unplanned construction.

**Proposals:**
- Turn the surrounding green belt into a connected green network
- Develop green corridors along the Mahmoudeya Canal and the waterfronts
- Make Alexandria a "sponge city" with rain gardens, permeable paving and tree box filters
- Revitalise public parks such as Al-Shalalat Park and support urban farming

**Examples:** Ottawa's Greenbelt, Meewasin Valley and Toronto greenways in Canada, green roofs in Singapore, cycling in Copenhagen.

## Mobility

**Challenges:** a sharp fall in public transport use, heavy car dependency, congestion on the Corniche, old buses and trams, and poor conditions for walking and cycling.

**Proposals:**
- A Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system on the Corniche and Abu Qir Street, plus discounted fares
- Smart traffic management with sensors and adaptive traffic signals
- Electric buses and trams, cycling lanes, pedestrian zones and low-emission zones

**Examples:** TransMilenio BRT in Bogotá, smart traffic management in Singapore, green transport in Oslo.

## Energy

**Challenges:** electricity generation depends heavily on natural gas, and transport and industry produce high emissions.

**Proposals:**
- Expand solar and wind power to reach 42% renewable electricity by 2035
- Improve energy efficiency through smart grids, smart meters and efficient building standards
- Add nuclear power through the El Dabaa Nuclear Power Plant west of Alexandria

**Examples:** Benban Solar Park in Egypt.

## Water

**Challenges:** Egypt is below the UN water poverty line, depends heavily on the Nile, and faces added pressure from the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD) and climate change.

**Proposals:**
- Demand side: fix network leaks, reform tariffs, use water-efficient appliances and improve irrigation
- Supply side: seawater desalination for drinking water and recycled wastewater for non-potable uses
- Power desalination plants with solar energy to reduce costs and emissions

**Examples:** large desalination plants in Agadir and Casablanca, Morocco.

## Circularity

**Challenges:** a low recycling rate, much waste still dumped in the open, gaps between urban and rural waste collection, and limited public awareness.

**Proposals:**
- Upgrade recycling and sorting facilities
- Build waste-to-energy plants
- Use public-private partnerships and promote second-hand goods, repair and refurbishment

**Context:** Egypt's Waste Management Law No. 202 of 2020 and the National Solid Waste Management Programme, co-financed by the EU.

## Conclusion

Alexandria's challenges are connected: rapid growth, informal housing, coastal risk, pollution and dependence on fossil fuels. Solving them needs cooperation between government, the private sector and local communities.

---

## Key targets proposed

- Meet the WHO guideline of 9 m² of green space per person by 2040
- Raise public transport ridership to 40% by 2030
- Generate 42% of electricity from renewable sources by 2035
- Protect the coast against a projected sea level rise of 50 cm by 2050
